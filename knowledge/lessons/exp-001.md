# Lessons — Experiment 001

**Source:** `experiments/001_rsi_btc_4h/`
**Date:** 2026-09-14
**Tags:** exp-001-baseline, exp-001-wfo, exp-001-bootstrap

---

## Summary

RSI(14/30/50) mean reversion on BTC-USD 4H, per pre-registered spec:
- Causal/leakage: PASS
- Walk-forward (single 50/50): FAIL
- Bootstrap: INCONCLUSIVE

Final: **no robust evidence of predictive performance.**

---

## Process lessons

### 1. Provenance is a discipline, not a checkbox

Every stage (baseline, WFO, bootstrap) initially produced numbers from a
dirty tree. In every case, we:
1. committed the script,
2. wiped the dirty artifacts,
3. reran from a clean tree,
4. confirmed float-point exact match,
5. committed the frozen result.

This took extra cycles but produced an auditable chain. Any future
experiment should **commit the runner before the first run**, not after.

### 2. Pre-registered criteria must be applied literally

Pre-registration said: `OOS Sharpe < 0 → FAIL`. The result was `-0.757`.
There was no room for "but it's close to zero" or "maybe with a
different split". The protocol's value comes from this rigidity.

### 3. Layer independence matters

A WFO FAIL does not invalidate the leakage PASS. A bootstrap
INCONCLUSIVE does not rescue the WFO FAIL. Each layer answers a
different question. Collapsing them into a single verdict would destroy
information.

### 4. "Tested pathway not detected" ≠ "no leakage exists"

The leakage suite tested specific pathways (target-leak, cross
semantics, future-feature, random-walk null). Passing them means those
pathways are clean. It does not mean the pipeline is universally clean.
This language discipline is load-bearing for the research program.

### 5. Small sample, weak inference

31 baseline trades over ~2 years of 4H bars is a small sample. Even
the IS numbers (15 trades, Sharpe 1.56) should not be over-interpreted.
The 93% IS win rate is a warning, not a signal: it suggests the IS
sample was dominated by favorable regime conditions.

### 6. Bootstrap block length collapse is informative

Block length = 1 from the ACF-based estimator means the observed
portfolio returns showed no detectable serial dependence at lag-1
under the chosen threshold. This is a finding to record, not to
hide. It tells us the effective sample size for the mean-return
estimator is close to the raw count.

### 7. A research system should make it easier to reject a hypothesis
   than to rationalize it

This is the transferable meta-lesson from Project 1 made operational in
Project 2. The multi-layer pipeline (baseline → leakage → WFO → bootstrap)
was designed so that:

- each layer has a pre-registered decision rule,
- layers do not rescue each other,
- a negative verdict closes the experiment without room for tuning.

The system produced a rejection on the first real experiment. That is not
a failure of the strategy alone — it is evidence that the research process
itself functions as intended.

---

## What transferred from Project 1

- Negative results require as much rigor as positive ones.
- Sympy-style accounting or invariance checks catch bugs that unit
  tests miss.
- A single clean backtest is never proof of edge.
- Multiple testing / post-hoc selection is the silent killer.

Project 1's lesson ("AI/indicator assistance requires independent
verification") transferred directly: the evaluator's role in this
experiment was exactly that verification layer.

---

## What is new in Project 2

- **Multi-layer validation** as a first-class pipeline (leakage, WFO,
  bootstrap) — not an afterthought.
- **Frozen data with SHA-256 manifest** — data integrity is now an
  invariant, not a hope.
- **Reproducibility via clean-tree rerun** — becomes a required step,
  not a luxury.
- **Pre-registration as a lock** — specification changes require a new
  experiment, not a modification of the current one.

timeframe in response to these results. Any such change constitutes a

Move to the next pre-registered experiment when the Research Lab
